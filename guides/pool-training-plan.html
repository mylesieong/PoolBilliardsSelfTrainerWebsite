Building a pool training plan

Splitting a week of practice · Updated August 2026

A training plan is not a schedule of when you will be at the table. It is a decision, made in advance, about what each visit is for — so that the choice is not being made by whatever you feel like doing after a bad day at work.

Start from the hours you really have

Plan for the worst realistic week, not the best one. Three hours a week that you actually complete beats eight hours that you abandon by Wednesday. If a session gets missed, the plan should absorb it without collapsing.

Split the time, roughly, like this

ShareAreaWhy
40%Cue-ball control and positionThe largest gap for most players above beginner level
25%Accuracy and potting under repetitionMaintains the base; degrades fast when neglected
25%Match play / solo racksWhere decisions are trained, and where drills get tested
10%Your worst shotDeliberately unpleasant; this is where the fastest gains hide

Beginners should invert the first two: accuracy first, position second. Advanced players often need to raise match play, because their drills are far cleaner than their games.

Rotate focus weekly, not daily

Skills consolidate over days. Switching focus every session means you are always starting over. Pick a theme for the week — say, draw distance control — and let it appear in every session that week, alongside the maintenance work.

Fix the benchmark before the week starts

Choose one standardised test and run it on the first and last session of a training block. Everything in between can vary; the benchmark cannot. Without a fixed measurement, a four-week block gives you four weeks of impressions.

A four-week block that works. Week 1: benchmark, then position drills. Week 2: position drills at longer distance. Week 3: position under match conditions (solo racks, no resets). Week 4: reduce volume, re-run the benchmark. Then choose the next theme from what the numbers say, not from what you enjoyed.

Write down the workload, not the intention

"Work on draw" is not a plan. "Draw ladder, 3 sets × 10, two diamonds" is. The second one can be completed, scored, and compared with last week; the first one cannot fail, which is exactly what makes it useless.

Common questions

How many days a week should I practise pool?

Three to four short sessions is the sweet spot for most amateurs. Two is maintenance; five or more only pays off if the sessions are structured, otherwise you are grooving whatever habits you already have.

Should league players practise differently?

Yes — weight match play higher during the season and drills higher out of season. In season, your scarcest resource is fresh decisions, not fresh technique.

How long before a plan shows results?

Expect four to six weeks before a benchmark moves meaningfully. Anything faster is usually a good day, and anything slower usually means the plan has no measurement in it.
